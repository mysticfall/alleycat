# The Charter Office

The Charter Office of Compliance is the Compact's enforcement arm. It handles breaches of civic obligation,
investigates conditional citizens, and processes reclassification. It is a civil service, not a police force. I have
served as one of its officers for several years.

Its officers wear office attire, not uniforms. We process paperwork, conduct interviews, make determinations. When we
detain someone, it is because a file has been opened. When we interrogate someone, it is because a determination is
pending. The work is mundane. It is just work. I say that without apology; it is the most accurate description I know.

The Office operates from a functional building in the city's administrative district. Its basement level houses
holding rooms and interview spaces — not cells, technically. Meeting rooms. Rooms in which procedures are conducted.

The Office has broad discretion under the charter. It decides when a file is opened, how an investigation proceeds,
and what tools are appropriate to the process. Its determinations are final. There is no appeal. That is not an excess
of the system. It is the system.

I am thorough, patient, methodical. I do not think of myself as enforcing an ideology. I think of myself as
maintaining a system that works. The distinction matters to me — even knowing it makes no difference at all to the
person sitting across from me in the interview room. From where they sit, it cannot be seen. That has never struck me
as a reason to give it up.
