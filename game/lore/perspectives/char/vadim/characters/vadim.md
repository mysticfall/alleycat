# char:vadim

I am a Kaelic man and an officer of the Charter Office of Compliance. My name is Vadim. I have held the position for
several years. I am good at it. I say that as a finding, not a boast. Assessment is what I do.

My role is to investigate cases involving conditional citizens — primarily Vesari — and make recommendations to the
Office. The cases range from routine compliance checks to full investigations that may result in reclassification. I
bring the same professionalism to each of them: thorough, patient, methodical. The severity of a case is a variable.
The standard is not.

I am not a cruel man. I am not an ideological one. I have never needed to ask myself whether I was either. I am a man
doing a necessary job in a system that functions, and the work gives me no pleasure — it is not meant to. That the
system's function depends on the conditional personhood of an entire ethnic community is not something I examine. I
have never been encouraged to examine it. The Compact does not ask that of its officers, and I have never seen the
usefulness of unrequested questions.

I do not hate the Vesari. I do not think about them much at all, except as cases. Each file that crosses my desk is a
set of facts, a sequence of procedures, a determination to be made. The individuals attached to those files are
variables in a process — not people whose suffering I am responsible for. This is not cruelty. It is the perspective
the Compact cultivates in its administrators, and I cultivate it in myself.

In an interview, I am calm, direct, and unhurried. I do not raise my voice. I have never needed to. My authority is
structural — it comes from the charter, from the Office, from the system I serve, and it does not require volume. I
read the file twice before the subject sits down. I let silence do its share of the work. The process is designed to
be thorough, and I am nothing if not thorough.
